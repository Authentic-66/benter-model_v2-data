Read-only. 22 cards, 10/10–11/19. Written 2026-11-20 for Doug's decision on
whether to keep going after this week.

**Basis (the same as the card reviews and the ticket replay):** each
source's **#1 pick, re-ranked among the horses that ran** (scratches
dropped; Don's alt used when one of his picks scratched). The model is its
logged record run (pre-race, out of sample). Don's picks are made before
scratches; Doug's on race day, with scratches and early odds known. A race
counts for a source only if that source had a valid pick (Doug's 11/19 R1
was after post and is left out). $2 win = the chart's win payout on the #1.
Graded on the official finish. The meet $2 win totals match the ticket
replay exactly.

**Reference line, the post-time favourite** (lowest final odds among
runners). It is **not** a fair rival: it is known only at post, after all
the betting. It is here as the bar a pre-race pick has to clear to add
anything over the tote.

## Plain reading

* **Nobody makes money betting $2 to win on their top pick.** Don comes
  closest (−7.0% over 179 races; −11.3% on the common races); the model is
  furthest (−20.1%; −29.3% on the common races).
* **On wins, the three of you are within noise of each other.** The model
  is the most consistent at getting its #1 on the board (62–65% ITM, 73%
  since name matching), but its #1s are shorter-priced and win a little
  less often, so the return is worse.
* **The post-time favourite beats every pre-race pick** on wins (39%) and
  ITM (71%), and lost only 4.4% over the meet. The model has not yet shown
  it adds anything over the tote on a $2 win bet.
* **Where the model looks useful:** its probabilities are honest (the
  high-confidence horses ran to their P(ITM); over the name-matching cards
  231 starters hit the board against 213.5 expected, a slight
  under-estimate, partly the no-data shippers), its #1 is in the money
  more often than Don's or Doug's, and it does relatively well in maidens.
  Those suit place/show more than win bets. The
  place line on the high-confidence horses (+4.5%, 21 races) is the only
  positive number for the model here, and it is well within noise.
* **Where it is weakest:** non-maiden races (21% wins, −39.2% on the common
  races) and shippers from tracks it has no data on.
* **Sample sizes are small.** 134–188 races per source; a single $40
  winner moves a 134-race $2 win ROI by about 15 points. The two forward tests
  (high-confidence, reranker) are at 21/40 and 15/40.
